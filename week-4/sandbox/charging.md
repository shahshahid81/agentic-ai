# U.S. Public EV Charging Landscape for Fleet Planning

## How many public charging points are there?
The U.S. has **roughly 170,000 public charging ports** at **about 65,000 public charging locations**. The most widely used public source for this count is the U.S. Department of Energy’s **Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC)** / **Alternative Fueling Station Locator**.

For fleet planning, the **port** count matters more than the station count because a single site can have multiple connectors and charge multiple vehicles at once. In practice, the public network is growing quickly, but availability can still be uneven by region, with denser coverage along major corridors and in larger metro areas.

## Network option: Tesla Supercharger
**Tesla Supercharger** is the most established fast-charging network in the U.S. It stands out for broad highway coverage, generally strong uptime, and a simple driver experience. As the industry shifts toward **NACS / SAE J3400**, access is also expanding beyond Tesla vehicles.

For a fleet, Superchargers can be a good fit for long-distance travel and en-route charging. The main caveat is compatibility: access depends on the vehicle, the connector standard, and whether a site supports non-Tesla charging.

## Network option: Electrify America
**Electrify America** is one of the largest open public DC fast-charging networks in the U.S. It is especially relevant for mixed-brand fleets because it supports public access and has wide coverage in many metropolitan and highway markets.

For fleet use, Electrify America can serve as a practical backup to depot charging and a useful option for corridor driving. Fleets should still validate the specific sites and routes they expect to use, since charger availability and reliability can vary by location.
